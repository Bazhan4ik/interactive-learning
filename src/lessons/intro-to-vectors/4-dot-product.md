The **dot product** multiplies two vectors and produces a *number*. Multiply matching components and add:

**a** · **b** = aₓbₓ + aᵧbᵧ

For example, (3, 2) · (4, −1) = 12 − 2 = 10.

### What it means

The same number can be computed from lengths and the angle θ between the vectors:

**a** · **b** = |**a**| |**b**| cos θ

So the dot product measures how much two vectors **point the same way**:

- **positive** when θ is less than 90°
- **zero** when they are perpendicular
- **negative** when θ is more than 90°

### Projection

The dashed line drops **a** straight onto the line of **b**, like a shadow cast by a light shining directly above it. The highlighted segment is the **projection** of **a** onto **b**, and

**a** · **b** = |**b**| × (signed length of the shadow)

Drag the vectors and watch the shadow shrink to nothing as θ reaches 90°, then flip to the other side.

The dot product shows up everywhere: in physics, the work done by a force is **F** · **d**. In graphics, it decides how brightly light hits a surface.
